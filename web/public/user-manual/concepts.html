Core Concepts — Options Basics

Options are contracts that give the buyer the right (but not obligation) to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price before a deadline.

Calls and Puts

A call profits when the underlying rises above the strike. A put profits when the underlying falls below the strike.

TermMeaning
Strike price (K)The agreed buy/sell price in the contract.
Expiration date (DTE)The date the contract expires. DTE = days to expiry.
PremiumThe price paid by the buyer to the seller.
ITM / ATM / OTMIn/At/Out of the money — whether exercising the option now would be profitable.

Black-Scholes Model

The foundational options pricing formula. It derives a theoretical fair value for European-style options based on current price, strike, time to expiry, risk-free rate, and implied volatility.

C = S·N(d₁) − K·e−rT·N(d₂)
d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)·T] / (σ·√T) ← Standardized distance to strike
d₂ = d₁ − σ·√T
Investopedia — Black-Scholes Wikipedia — Black-Scholes

Strategy Types

OptionsLab evaluates six multi-leg options strategies. All payoffs are computed at expiry (European-style).

Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike. Profits when the underlying makes a large move in either direction. Best entered when IV is low (cheap options).

Payoff = max(S−K, 0) + max(K−S, 0) − Premium
Break-even: K − Premium  |  K + Premium
Investopedia — Straddle

Bull Call Spread (Vertical Spread)

Buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call. Capped upside, capped downside. Directional bullish, lower cost than outright long call.

Max gain = (Short − Long) − Net debit
Max loss = Net debit paid
Break-even = Long strike + Net debit
Investopedia — Bull Call Spread

Bear Put Spread (Vertical Spread)

Buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put. Directional bearish with capped risk and reward.

Break-even = Long strike − Net debit
Investopedia — Bear Put Spread

Iron Condor

Combine a bear call spread and a bull put spread. Profits when the underlying stays between the inner strikes. Premium-selling strategy — best in high-IV environments.

Strikes: Put-Long < Put-Short < Call-Short < Call-Long
Max gain = Net credit collected
Break-even: Put-Short − credit  |  Call-Short + credit
Investopedia — Iron Condor

Calendar Spread

Buy a longer-dated option and sell a shorter-dated option at the same strike. Profits from the faster time decay of the short leg and IV expansion. Approximated as European in this tool.

Approx break-even: Strike ± (Net debit / 2)
Investopedia — Calendar Spread

Covered Call & Protective Put (Backtests only)

Available in the Backtests module via Backtrader. Not evaluated by the strategy engine (which works with multi-leg payoff functions).

  • Covered call: long stock + short call. Income strategy, caps upside.
  • Protective put: long stock + long put. Insurance strategy, limits downside.

Volatility Metrics

Historical Volatility (HV)

The annualized standard deviation of log returns over a rolling window. Measures how much the stock has been moving. Available at 10, 20, 30, and 60-day windows.

HVn = σ(log(Closet / Closet-1), n days) × √252
252 = trading days per year
Investopedia — Historical Volatility Wikipedia — Volatility (finance)

Implied Volatility (IV)

The volatility value that makes a Black-Scholes model price match the market's actual option price. It represents the market's forward expectation of volatility. When IV > HV, options are pricing in more movement than has been realized — often a premium-selling opportunity.

Investopedia — Implied Volatility tastytrade — IV guide

IV Rank (IVR)

Where today's IV sits within the past year's range, expressed as 0–100.

IVR = (IVnow − IV52w low) / (IV52w high − IV52w low) × 100

IVR 0 = IV at yearly low · IVR 100 = IV at yearly high · IVR 50 = exactly in the middle.

IVRInterpretationPreferred strategy class
≥ 60High — options expensiveSell premium: iron condors, credit spreads
35 – 59Moderate — neutralDepends on directional forecast
< 35Low — options cheapBuy premium: straddles, debit spreads
tastytrade — IVR & IVP

IV Percentile (IVP)

The percentage of trading days in the lookback window where IV was lower than today. More robust than IVR because it is not distorted by single outlier spikes.

IVP = count(IVhistory < IVnow) / count(IVhistory) × 100
Option Alpha — IVP

Expected Move (1σ, 30-day)

The market's implied price range over 30 calendar days. At one sigma, ~68% of outcomes should land inside this range — the same concept as the straddle price approximation.

EM = Current Price × IV × √(30 / 252)
tastytrade — Expected Move

Forecasting Models

ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average)

A classical time-series model. Fits a linear combination of the stock's own past values and past errors. Fast, interpretable, and good at capturing trend and mean-reversion. OptionsLab defaults to an AR(1) order — meaning the best single-step predictor is yesterday's value.

AR(1): yt = c + φ·yt-1 + εt
φ = autoregressive coefficient, ε = noise
Investopedia — ARIMA Wikipedia — ARIMA statsmodels docs

Gradient Boosting (GB)

A tree-ensemble model trained on lagged features (past close prices, returns, SMAs, realized volatility). Better at capturing non-linear patterns but requires more data than ARIMA and is slower to fit. Uses scikit-learn's HistGradientBoostingRegressor.

scikit-learn — HistGradientBoosting Wikipedia — Gradient Boosting

Kelly Criterion & Position Sizing

The Kelly Formula

Derived by John Kelly (Bell Labs, 1956), this formula computes the fraction of capital that maximizes the long-run growth rate of a trading account given a known edge.

f* = (p × b − q) / b

p = win rate (e.g. 0.55)
q = loss rate = 1 − p
b = avg_win / avg_loss (the odds ratio)

Example: p = 0.55, avg win = $500, avg loss = $300
b = 500/300 = 1.667  →  f* = (0.55×1.667 − 0.45) / 1.667 ≈ 0.55 (55%)

55% is way too aggressive in practice. Full Kelly leads to catastrophic drawdowns whenever your edge estimate is even slightly off. Most professional traders use Half-Kelly or less.
Wikipedia — Kelly Criterion Investopedia — Kelly Criterion

Half-Kelly (Recommended)

Use f* / 2 as your position size. Research shows Half-Kelly retains ~75% of the long-run optimal growth rate while dramatically reducing drawdown and providing a safety margin for edge estimation error.

f_half = f* / 2
Fortune's Formula (book)

Fixed Fractional Sizing

A simpler alternative: risk a fixed percentage of capital (e.g. 1–2%) on any single trade, regardless of estimated edge. More robust when edge estimates are uncertain.

Dollar risk = Capital × risk_%
Units = Dollar risk / (Max loss per unit)
Investopedia — Fixed Fractional

Break-Even Analysis

The break-even price is the underlying price at expiry where a strategy's net P&L = 0 after accounting for the premium paid or credit received. Always compute break-even before entering a trade — it tells you how much the stock needs to move in your favor just to cover costs.

StrategyBreak-even formulaExample
Long StraddleStrike ± PremiumK=100, prem=$4 → BE at $96 and $104
Bull Call SpreadLong strike + Net debitLong 98, debit $2 → BE at $100
Bear Put SpreadLong strike − Net debitLong 102, debit $2 → BE at $100
Iron CondorPut-short − credit; Call-short + creditPS=97, CS=103, credit=$1.50 → BE at $95.50 and $104.50
Calendar SpreadStrike ± (debit / 2) — approximationK=100, debit=$2 → BE ~$99 and ~$101

In OptionsLab, enter the premium in the Premium paid ($) field when evaluating a strategy. Break-even prices appear as amber badges in the results section.

Macro & Economic Data

Macroeconomic conditions affect volatility, sector rotation, and risk appetite. OptionsLab pulls data from three primary government sources:

FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data)

The most comprehensive free source for U.S. macro data — 800,000+ time series including GDP, CPI, interest rates, employment, and market indicators like VIX.

FRED website FRED API docs

BLS (Bureau of Labor Statistics)

Employment situation, CPI-U, wages, and productivity data. Updated monthly.

BLS website BLS API docs

BEA (Bureau of Economic Analysis)

National accounts, GDP by expenditure, corporate profits. Data is typically quarterly.

BEA website BEA API docs
How macro data improves forecasts When macro data is synced, enable "Macro enrichment" in the forecast form. The model forward-fills monthly indicators onto daily bars, giving the forecaster context like "current GDP trend" or "current CPI level" alongside price features.
